Frequently Asked Questions

Valentine's Day is observed on February 14 each year, so the weekday changes but the calendar date does not.

It is widely observed as a cultural or commercial occasion, but it is not generally treated as a statutory public holiday. Check your local holiday authority if leave or business hours matter.

What this countdown tells you

The countdown targets the next February 14. If this year's Valentine's Day has already passed, it automatically moves to February 14 of the following year. The large figure shows complete days remaining, while the smaller counters show the hours, minutes and seconds left inside the current partial day.

Useful planning examples

The exact day count can help if you want to book a restaurant before popular times disappear, order a personalised gift that has a production lead time, arrange flowers, organise childcare, plan a weekend away, or work out when an online order needs to be placed. It is also useful for businesses scheduling Valentine's promotions without manually counting calendar days.

Work backwards from February 14

A countdown answers “how long is left?”, but sometimes the more useful question is “what date should I start?”. For example, if a custom gift needs 21 days for production and delivery, use the Date Calculator to subtract 21 days from February 14. For a fixed interval between two chosen dates, use Days Between Dates.

Local time matters for online plans

This page reads your device's local clock. That is ideal for a local dinner or event, but not enough if you are arranging a midnight message, livestream or delivery across countries. February 14 begins at different moments around the world, so check the recipient's location with the World Clock when timing something internationally.
