How the New Zealand school year works

New Zealand schools and kura generally operate across four terms, but the start of Term 1 and the end of Term 4 can sit within official ranges rather than a single date for every school. That flexibility means a national term table is useful for planning, but the student's own school calendar is still the final check.

What the 2026 ranges mean

When a term shows a range rather than one fixed day, schools choose an opening or closing date that fits within the rules applying to that year. Families should therefore avoid assuming that another school nearby has exactly the same first or last day. The middle terms are usually easier to compare because their national dates are more tightly defined.

Planning around holidays and teacher-only days

Use the term ranges to sketch out family travel, childcare, work leave and visits, then add the school's teacher-only days and any local notices. A teacher-only day can create a one-day care gap even when the national term is underway, so those dates matter just as much as the main holiday blocks for day-to-day planning.

Public holidays can affect attendance

New Zealand's school calendar also interacts with national and regional public holidays. If a holiday falls close to a term boundary, check the actual school notice rather than trying to infer attendance from the term table alone. Local events and school-specific arrangements can also change the practical schedule.

Best way to use this reference

Start with the official Ministry of Education term dates, note the broad term pattern, then confirm the exact opening and closing dates with the student's school or kura. For flights, accommodation, custody arrangements or paid childcare, make that final school-level check before committing money.
