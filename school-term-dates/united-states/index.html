Dates are set locally

The United States does not have one national school calendar. Districts and schools publish their own opening dates, breaks, staff days and final days.

Why US school dates vary so much

Public school calendars in the United States are usually determined by states, districts and local boards rather than a single federal calendar. Two districts in the same state can start the year on different dates, schedule different professional-development days and finish at different times. Private and charter schools can vary again. For that reason, a national list is useful only as background and should not be treated as the student's actual attendance calendar.

What to look for on the district calendar

Check the first and last student attendance days, Thanksgiving break, winter break, spring break and any fall break used locally. Also look for teacher workdays, conference days, weather make-up days and early-release dates. Those details are often the difference between a calendar that looks right and one that is actually useful for childcare or travel.

Real planning examples

Families can use the district calendar to arrange camps, care, annual leave, visits with relatives and travel. If children live across two households or attend different schools, compare both calendars before making bookings. University calendars are separate again, so a household with school-age children and college students may need to coordinate more than one timetable.

Weather and emergency changes

Some districts build snow or emergency days into the school year, while others add missed days later. A published end date can therefore change after severe weather or another closure. If a booking depends on the final day of school, check the district again closer to the date rather than relying on a copy saved months earlier.

Best way to verify a date

Use the school or district's current published calendar and confirm unusual dates with the school office when necessary. This page helps you locate the right source and understand what to check; it does not replace a district's official calendar.
