Frequently Asked Questions

St Patrick's Day is March 17 every year.

Its legal holiday status depends on location. It is a public holiday in the Republic of Ireland and Northern Ireland, while elsewhere it is often celebrated without being a statutory day off. Check the relevant local authority for workplace or school closures.

What this St Patrick's Day countdown measures

The page targets the next March 17. Before that date it counts down to the current year's observance; once March 17 has passed, it automatically moves to the following year. The day figure is paired with hours, minutes and seconds so the countdown stays useful as the date gets close.

Where the countdown is genuinely useful

It can help community groups, pubs, schools, performers and families work backwards from March 17 when organising a parade outing, themed event, fundraiser, travel booking or promotional campaign. If a venue needs a final headcount two weeks beforehand, or printed material needs to be ordered a month earlier, the day count gives you a quick sense of how much runway is left.

March 17 is fixed, but the weekday changes

Unlike Easter, St Patrick's Day always falls on March 17. What changes is the weekday, which can matter quite a lot for event planning. A Saturday celebration may be organised differently from a Tuesday one. The target date shown by the tool includes the weekday so you do not have to look it up separately.

Public-holiday rules are location-specific

Do not use a cultural countdown as proof that a workplace, school or business will be closed. Holiday entitlements and opening hours depend on jurisdiction. For formal planning, verify local rules and use the Next Public Holiday tool as a starting point. If you need to calculate an earlier preparation deadline, subtract the required lead time with the Date Calculator.
