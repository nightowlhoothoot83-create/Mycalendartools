Printable Calendar

Generate one clean month at a time, then print it or save it as a PDF.

What this printable calendar actually creates

Choose a month and year and the page builds a standard Sunday-to-Saturday monthly grid with the correct number of days and the right starting weekday. If you select the current month, today's date is highlighted on screen. The calendar itself is deliberately uncluttered so you can write on the printed copy rather than fighting a page full of decorations or pre-filled events.

Useful ways to use a blank month

A paper month is still useful when a phone calendar is too fiddly. Families can pin one to the fridge for appointments and school events, students can mark assignment deadlines, shift workers can colour-code rosters, and a small business can sketch out bookings, content dates or payment deadlines. Because the page can generate past or future years, it is also handy for checking what weekday a date occupied in another month.

Print it cleanly

After generating the month you want, use the Print button and check your browser's preview before committing paper. If the calendar feels cramped, landscape orientation may give you more writing room; if it is too large, reduce the print scale slightly. Most desktop browsers also provide a “Save as PDF” destination, which gives you a portable copy without needing a separate PDF generator.

What the calendar does not add automatically

This is a blank calendar grid. It does not assume your country, state, school, religion or workplace, so it does not automatically mark every public holiday or school break. That is intentional: those dates differ between jurisdictions. Use the Public Holiday and School Holidays pages to check relevant dates, then write or annotate the ones that apply to you.

Monthly view versus the whole year

Use this page when you want room to write inside individual days. If you need to see how months line up across an entire year, switch to the Year at a Glance tool instead. The two views solve different planning problems rather than duplicating one another.
